Market Reversal

Kia EV9 2026: Near-New Units Are Holding Strong

After months of watching the 2024 and 2025 EV9s crater in value, the 2026 cohort tells a different story. Near-new 2026 units — many with under 5,000 miles — are trading at modest discounts to MSRP rather than deep-discount depreciation. The GT-Line especially has seen its floor rise by nearly $10K.

Spec Corrections

Six Data Errors Fixed This Cycle

This refresh found a higher-than-usual number of substantive spec errors. Two were copy/paste errors from related trims; four were charging type fields that were set incorrectly when the data was first added.

✗

Lucid Gravity Dream Edition — Battery & Range

battery_kwh: 89 → 123 kWh  ·  range_mi: 420 → 440 mi
The 89 kWh figure belonged to the Touring trim; Dream Edition shares the 123 kWh pack with the Grand Touring. Physics now make sense: same battery, slightly less range than GT due to extra motor weight.

✗

Rivian R1S 2025 Adventure Dual Standard — Battery

battery_kwh: 109.4 → 92.5 kWh
The Standard battery pack on Gen 2 R1S is 92.5 kWh (LFP chemistry). The 109.4 figure is the Large pack. The 2026 Dual Standard already had 92.5 correctly; this was a 2025 holdover error.

✗

Cadillac VISTIQ — Charging Type (all 4 trims)

NACS → CCS1 (Luxury, Sport, Premium Luxury, Platinum)
The 2026 VISTIQ uses CCS1 across all trims. The NACS transition for Cadillac starts with the 2027 model year. Three trims showed NACS incorrectly; a fourth showed “CCS” without the 1.

✗

Mercedes-Benz EQS SUV — Charging Type (2025 & 2026)

NACS → CCS1 (+NACS adpt) for all 2025 trims; same for 3 mismatched 2026 trims
The EQS SUV (all years on this platform) uses CCS1. The 2026 MY adds the NACS adapter included in the box, but the port is still CCS1. Only the new 2026 CLA has native NACS in the Mercedes lineup.
