Depreciation Alert

Kia EV9 2024: The Collapse Has Spread to Wind & Land

A month ago we noted that 2026 EV9 prices were weak. Now the 2024 cohort is telling a bleaker story. The Wind and Land trims — which used to anchor the mid-range used market — are now trading 40–45% below their original MSRPs. KBB private party values confirm this isn’t just a bad-listing problem.

2024 Trim MSRP (new) Old Range New Range
Light RWD $56,395 $24–31K $27–34K
Wind ~$60K $36–43K $31–39K
Land ~$64K $41–44K $33–40K
GT-Line ~$70K $37–49K $37–45K
What’s happening: The EV9 launched with strong early demand and healthy initial resale. But a combination of rapid MSRP cuts, increased supply, and softening EV appetite has compacted the entire model year’s residual values. The 2024 Land — originally ~$64K new — now starts at $33K used. That’s a 48% drop in under two years.
Repriced

Volvo EX90 Performance Ultra: Down $9K on the Low End

The EX90 Performance Ultra launched at nearly $98K new. One year later, KBB pegs resale at $56,200 — a 37% first-year depreciation. Our previous range of $62–72K was significantly overstating the market.

−9K

EX90 Performance Ultra (both 6 & 7-seat)

Pre-owned range: $62,000–$72,000 → $53,000–$63,000  ·  KBB resale: $56,200

Context: This puts a year-old EX90 Performance Ultra ($53–63K) now price-competitive with a new Kia EV9 Land or IONIQ 9 SE — vehicles with arguably more practical features for family buyers. That’s a compelling value proposition for used luxury shoppers.

Lexus TZ Joins the Watchlist

Officially revealed May 6, 2026. The Lexus TZ is the luxury-brand sibling to the Toyota Highlander EV, built on the same Georgetown, KY line. It’s the 19th vehicle on the site and 8th on the Watchlist — expected on sale by end of 2026.

+

Lexus TZ — Base (expected)

77 kWh · AWD · Range TBD (~260 est) · MSRP ~$70K+ · NACS · 150 kW DC fast

+

Lexus TZ — Select (expected)

95.8 kWh · AWD · 300 mi range · MSRP ~$80K+ · 35 min 10–80% · Mark Levinson 21-speaker

What’s notable: The TZ fills a gap between the Volvo EX90 (~$60–80K) and the Lucid Gravity ($100K+). It lands squarely in the premium-but-not-ultra tier. Two things stand out: DIRECT4 AWD is standard on every grade (no base FWD option unlike the Highlander), and there is no frunk — a notable omission Electrek flagged at reveal. Trims and final pricing arrive later in 2026.
Resale Anomaly

Lucid Gravity GT: Used Prices at (or Above) MSRP

While nearly every other EV on the list is shedding value fast, the Lucid Gravity Grand Touring is doing the opposite. New MSRP is $100,750 — and used listings are ranging from $96K to $120K, with some examples selling at a modest premium.

↑

Lucid Gravity Grand Touring

Pre-owned range revised: $95–110K → $97–115K  ·  TrueCar: real listings $96K–$120K

Why: Supply is genuinely constrained. Lucid has limited production capacity, and early adopters aren’t parting with their cars cheaply. This is the only vehicle in the 3-row EV space currently appreciating in the used market.

XLE vs. Limited: Both the XLE AWD and Limited AWD use the 95.8 kWh battery, but the Limited ekes out 320 mi vs. 270 mi for the XLE — likely due to wheel size differences (the Limited presumably gets more aerodynamic 20” wheels vs. larger options on XLE). Not a huge deal for most buyers, but worth knowing before the vehicle goes on sale.
